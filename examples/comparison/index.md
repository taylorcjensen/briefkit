---
title: Picking a delivery format for a decision brief
description: A worked comparison of a static report site, PDF, and shared document.
---

> **Pick a static report site when the reader needs to scan a decision, follow sources, and move between sections.** Export a PDF when a fixed, offline copy matters more. Keep a shared document for live collaborative drafting, not as the final decision artifact.

This is a reusable example, not a claim that one format always wins. Replace the conditions with your reader's constraints before making the call.

## Comparison at a glance

| Option | Best for | Avoid if | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Static report site | Navigable, source-linked brief with tables and sections | Readers cannot access a browser or hosting location | Decide how revisions and access will be handled |
| PDF | Fixed copy, printing, and offline distribution | Readers need to explore long source trails or interact | Links may work, but pagination makes updates costly |
| Shared document | Concurrent drafting and comments | You need a stable snapshot of the decision | Permissions and version history need an owner |

## Decision rules

1. **Can every reader open the chosen location?** If not, deliver an offline copy as well.
2. **Will the brief change after review?** Name a version and owner, regardless of format.
3. **Do readers need to inspect evidence?** Keep citations adjacent to each factual claim; use a navigable source section.

## Risks to resolve

| Risk | Warning sign | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Permission mismatch | Reviewer asks for access | Test with the intended audience before sharing |
| Stale decision | Multiple versions circulate | Put a date and owner on each release |
| Citation drift | Source links no longer resolve | Save the source title, date, and relevant excerpt |

## Next action

Ask the decision owner which of **offline access**, **collaborative editing**, or **source navigation** is non-negotiable. Build the first deliverable in that format and offer the others only as needed.

## Sources and assumptions

This is a format-selection example, not a sourced market survey. [Briefkit's authoring guide](https://github.com/taylorcjensen/briefkit#readme) describes the static-site workflow; assess PDF and document tooling against your organization's actual constraints. The comparison entries are judgments, not measured performance claims.
